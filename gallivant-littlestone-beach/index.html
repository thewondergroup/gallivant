Gallivant Littlestone Beach

We’ve begun the complete restoration of Romney Bay House, a house by Clough Williams-Ellis on a sand dune above the sea. This is its story so far.

Become a FriendFollow the restoration
A house on the dunes

Our second house, and its first life

Along the coast from Camber, at Littlestone-on-Sea, a white house with a mansard roof and four tall columns looks straight out to sea. It was drawn up by Clough Williams-Ellis, the architect behind Portmeirion, for a Mrs M. E. Bray.

Today it’s known as Romney Bay House. Now we’re restoring it, room by room, to become Gallivant Littlestone Beach: our second house by the sea, with the same welcome you know from Camber.

From the drawing board to the dunes

How the house came to be

The architect

The man who built Portmeirion

Clough Williams-Ellis is best known for Portmeirion, the Italianate village he built on the Welsh coast. Romney Bay House is one of only a few examples of his work.

The columns, the roofline and the windows facing the sea are all his. Our job is to make them ready for many more summers.

From the archive

Summers at Littlestone

Beach days, motor cars, model aeroplanes on the lawn and a lot of sitting on the front steps.

Wish you were here

Littlestone-on-Sea, by postcard

A long sea wall to sit on, breakers to dodge, a parade of grand houses and The Grand Hotel. Littlestone was a seaside resort with ambitions.

The beach is still there, and so are the big skies over Romney Marsh. We think it’s ready for a second act.
