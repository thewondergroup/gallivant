A house on the dunes

Our second house, and its first life. Along the coast from Camber, at Littlestone-on-Sea, a white house with a mansard roof and four tall columns looks straight out to sea. It was drawn up by Clough Williams-Ellis, the architect behind Portmeirion, for a Mrs M. E. Bray.

Today it’s known as Romney Bay House. Now we’re restoring it, room by room, to become Gallivant Littlestone Beach: our second house by the sea, with the same welcome you know from Camber.

The build

How the house came to be. The house went up on bare sand, with nothing between it and the sea but marram grass.

Then and now

The same steps, the same view. Drag across to see the house as it was, and as we found it. The wings came later.

The architect

The man who built Portmeirion. Clough Williams-Ellis is best known for Portmeirion, the Italianate village he built on the Welsh coast. Romney Bay House is one of only a few examples of his work.

The columns, the roofline and the windows facing the sea are all his. Our job is to make them ready for many more summers.

From the archive

Summers at Littlestone. Beach days, motor cars, model aeroplanes on the lawn and a lot of sitting on the front steps.

Littlestone-on-Sea

Wish you were here. A long sea wall to sit on, breakers to dodge, a parade of grand houses and The Grand Hotel. Littlestone was a seaside resort with ambitions.

The beach is still there, and so are the big skies over Romney Marsh. We think it’s ready for a second act.

What happens next

Watch it come back to life. Work has started on site. Harry and Sigrid are sharing the restoration as it happens: the build, the problems, the finds, the makers and the rooms taking shape.
