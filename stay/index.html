Discover Kent and East Sussex

Medieval towns, great gardens and a moated castle, all within an hour.

Rye

The cobbles of Mermaid Street, the Ypres Tower and the view from the top of St Mary’s church tower, where you can see clear across the marsh to the sea. Henry James wrote at Lamb House; E.F. Benson turned the town into Tilling. Come for the antiques, the bookshops and a long lunch.

10 minutes by car, or 50 minutes on foot across the salt marsh.

Winchelsea

Edward I laid out this hilltop town on a grid in the 1280s, and it has barely grown since. Wander the quiet squares, look for the medieval wine cellars beneath the streets and find Spike Milligan’s headstone in the churchyard of St Thomas’s.

15 minutes by car.

Tenterden

A wide, tree-lined high street of weatherboarded and Georgian houses, known as the Jewel of the Weald. Board the Kent & East Sussex steam railway to Bodiam, or stop for a tasting at one of the vineyards on the edge of town.

30 minutes by car.

Great Dixter

Christopher Lloyd’s house and garden at Northiam: a medieval hall house restored by Lutyens, wrapped in the most daring planting in England. Meadows, topiary and the famous Long Border. Go in late spring or September.

25 minutes by car.

Sissinghurst Castle Garden

Vita Sackville-West and Harold Nicolson made a set of garden rooms around an Elizabethan tower. Climb the tower for the view, then sit in the White Garden. National Trust.

45 minutes by car.

Bodiam Castle

A 14th-century castle in the middle of its own moat, about as close to a child’s drawing of a castle as real life gets. Walk the ramparts, then take the steam train back towards Tenterden. National Trust.

30 minutes by car.

Six things worth doing

Interesting places to visit nearby

Dungeness

A shingle headland with two lighthouses, fishermen’s huts and Derek Jarman’s garden at Prospect Cottage, which you view from the road. Britain’s only desert, as it is often called, and the strangest, most beautiful walk on this coast.

25 minutes by car.

Battle Abbey and the 1066 battlefield

Stand where Harold fell. English Heritage has laid a walking trail across the battlefield, with the abbey William built in penance at the top. A good morning, followed by a pub lunch.

35 minutes by car.

Romney, Hythe & Dymchurch Railway

A one-third-size steam railway running 13 miles along the coast from Hythe to Dungeness. Ride it to the end of the line, climb the old lighthouse and come back.

25 minutes by car to New Romney.

Rye Harbour Nature Reserve

Salt marsh, shingle and lagoons where the River Rother meets the sea, with hides for watching terns, avocets and little egrets. Walk on to the ruins of Henry VIII’s Camber Castle.

15 minutes by car.

A vineyard tour

This corner of Kent and Sussex makes some of the best English sparkling wine. Book a tour and tasting at Oxney or Gusbourne then come back for English Wine at Five.

15–30 minutes by car.

Hastings Old Town

Tall black net huts on the Stade, Europe’s largest beach-launched fishing fleet, the funicular up East Hill and a street of junk shops and record shops. Pair it with Hastings Contemporary.

30 minutes by car.

Out for the evening

The Ypres Castle Inn, Rye

A 17th-century weatherboarded pub below the Ypres Tower, with a log fire, local ales and a big garden over the marsh. Named in the Telegraph’s Best Pubs in the UK 2026.

Pouring pints on Romney Marsh since 1410. Low beams, an inglenook fire and a smuggling past.

Hide and Fox, Saltwood

Two Michelin stars in a former village shop near Hythe, the first restaurant in Kent to earn a second. Tasting menus built on Kent produce. Book well ahead.

Coming to stay

The things people ask before they arrive.

All FAQsCall 01797 225 057
When can I check in and out?

Check-in is from 4pm and check-out by 11am. Arrive early, park up and have lunch or a beach walk while we finish your room.

How do I pay?

We take full payment at booking. For stays more than 90 days ahead, a 20% deposit secures the room and the balance is due 30 days before you arrive.

Is there a minimum stay?

Two nights at weekends. Call us and we’ll tell you if a single night has come free.

Can I bring children?

We welcome everyone aged 12 and over to stay and to dine at Harry’s in the evening. At lunch it’s 6 and over.

Can I bring my dog?

Yes, to a few dog-friendly rooms. One dog, no bigger than a Labrador, £25 a night, with a bed and bowl waiting. Dogs are welcome in the Sitting Room, Siggy’s, the Terrace and gardens, but not in Harry’s. Please tell us when you book.

Do the rooms have sea views?

No. The beach is a short walk over the dunes, and it goes on for miles.

Do you have twin or family rooms?

No. All rooms have a king or super king. The Baby and Large Hampton day beds can be made up for one extra adult, booked and paid in advance.

Which rooms have a bath?

All Luxury Garden, Baby Hampton and Large Hampton rooms, and one Garden room.

Is there parking?

Free for residents, right outside. Two EV chargers, which must be reserved in advance.

How do I get here by train?

Rye station is four miles away. Trains from London St Pancras run hourly, changing at Ashford International, in just over an hour. We can book you a taxi.

Is the hotel accessible?

There is step-free access to Harry’s, Siggy’s, the Snug and the Terrace, and most rooms are step-free. The garden paths are gravel. Email us and we’ll find the right room.
